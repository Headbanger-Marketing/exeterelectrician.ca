Panel and service upgrades in Exeter.

Adding a sub-panel to an Exeter home for a garage, basement or addition changes where cable must travel and where equipment must sit. Deciding both before surfaces are closed helps avoid reopening finished work.

A panel and an electrical service are different parts of the system

The panel distributes electricity to property circuits. The service is the incoming supply and associated equipment. Replacing a panel does not automatically increase the available supply. A Licensed Electrical Contractor should assess existing condition and demand, planned loads and the utility requirements before recommending equipment. Spare breaker spaces alone do not establish capacity.

Prepare the scope for your Exeter property

Sketch the path between the existing panel and the new area, noting any finished ceilings, closets or masonry the route would cross. List the equipment the new space will hold, such as a bench saw or a kiln.

Ask the contractor where the sub-panel will be mounted, how it stays reachable once finishes are on, and which parts of the route must stay open for the notification and inspection steps.

What should the assessment establish?

A sub-panel is being added in an Exeter home to feed a garage, basement or addition. Since a sub-panel only redistributes what the service provides, the supply is assessed for whether it can take on the new area at all. The planned loads, the feeder route and the mounting position are identified as well. The scope names who supplies and installs each part, any drilling or patching included, and which work must stay visible until the notification process is complete. Each of these points is given in plain wording and settled before surfaces are closed.

Ontario electrical safety

ESA says almost all electrical work needs a notification of work. If you hire someone for home electrical work, use a Licensed Electrical Contractor. Agree who files the notification and request the Certificate of Acceptance after ESA review.

Read the current ESA notification and inspection guidance, contractor hiring guidance. You can verify an electrical contractor with ESA before agreeing to work.

Questions before you agree to work

Is a garage sub-panel still realistic once the drywall is up?

It can sometimes be done, but the cable route may then need to cross finished surfaces. Ask the contractor to compare running the route now, while walls are open, with the extra patching and repair a later change could involve.

Does a sub-panel give the new space its own supply?

No. A sub-panel divides the supply already entering the home into more circuits for that area. Whether the existing supply can carry the added equipment is a separate assessment by a Licensed Electrical Contractor.
